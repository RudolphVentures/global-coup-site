How It Works

Global Coup runs five timed rounds per episode. Every question is built from a real, current geopolitical scenario — the format is the joke; the stakes are not.

Step 1

Five Rounds

Each round narrows the scenario — from broad regional context down to the single decision that would actually get made.

Step 2

45-Second Clock

Contestants answer against a visible countdown. Speed is scored separately from accuracy, same as the real thing.

Step 3

Weighted Scoring

Later rounds pay out more points. A strong finish beats a strong start — deliberately, since that's how most crises actually resolve.

Step 4

Live Leaderboard

Running point totals stay on screen the whole episode. No reveal at the end — you watch the coup happen in real time.

About

Global Coup is built and hosted by Trevor H. Rudolph, a Brussels-based technology and cybersecurity policy executive who spent years inside the White House Office of Management and Budget helping write federal cybersecurity policy, and now runs Rudolph Ventures. The show turns that same material — the questions policy staff actually argue about — into a format an audience can play along with.

It's the first of two branded shows from Rudolph Ventures. Its sibling, Memo: Random, takes the slower, conversational half of the same beat.

Dry. Not sarcastic.
The humor lives in the gap between high-stakes geopolitics and the gameshow format. Let reality do the comedy.
Smart. Not academic.
Assume the audience can handle complexity. Skip the jargon and the footnotes.
Competitive. Not combative.
Scores, rounds, timers — competition is the point. It's never mean-spirited.
